Energy balance

How the body gains and loses fat, what a calorie deficit actually does, and why weight loss slows down over time.

Body weight change is governed by the balance between energy intake and energy expenditure. When expenditure exceeds intake, the body increases its oxidation of stored fat for fuel. When body weight changes because of an energy imbalance, roughly 60 to 80% of the resulting change in body mass is typically body fat — the remainder is other tissue and body water.

Where does the fat actually go?

When fat is metabolised for energy, most of its mass leaves the body as exhaled carbon dioxide, with the remainder excreted as water. Fat is not "burned off" as heat and it is not excreted — it is exhaled.

Why loss slows down

Weight loss produces changes in resting and non-resting energy expenditure that are independent of changes in fat-free mass — this is called adaptive thermogenesis. It is a real effect that can slow further loss and favour regain, but it is a temporary metabolic adaptation, not permanent or irreversible metabolic damage.

Not all calories cost the same to process

The thermic effect of food — the energy spent digesting and processing what you eat — is highest for protein at about 20 to 30% of ingested energy, moderate for carbohydrate at about 5 to 10%, and lowest for fat at about 0 to 3%.

Design notes

Revised against DESIGN-ANALYSIS.md (sandow discipline, not decoration). Grouping comes entirely from background-tint shifts — --surface/--surface-2, now computed via color-mix() at ~4%/~5% off --bg, tightened from the previous draft's stronger 4–8% — plus spacing, never a border or shadow. The food-calculator total's tint box was removed outright: the running total now floats directly on the background exactly like Home's hero figure, since a screen that is pure numbers needs no card at all. The ToC list/search retain a container because they're one of the few things the analysis says legitimately keeps one (a form control and a navigational list host), but that container is now flat, not rounded.

Binary radius rule: one token, --radius-pill: 999px, applied only to actionable controls — icon buttons, the Add/Back CTA buttons, the citation marker, the search field, the gram-amount input. Everything else (rows, panels, the ToC list, the bottom-sheet corners, progress bars, the toast) is unrounded. This removed the three-tier --radius-lg/md/sm scale entirely.

Numeral-first, pushed further: the Home hero figure, the calculator total and both training stats now put the label beneath the number (was above in the previous draft) in a muted 0.75rem tone, with no divider between them — scale and weight alone do the separating. Hero figure grew to clamp(3.6rem, 19vw, 5.2rem); the calculator total to clamp(3rem, 15vw, 4rem).

Dot + word replaces every filled pill: the food-state tag, the ToC "New" tag, and the citation panel's tier/confidence tag were all filled accent or surface pills before — all three are now a small filled dot plus plain text, nothing else.

Icon-led inline stat rows were added wherever a repeated label/value pair existed: Home's protein/meals/training line, the calculator's protein/fat/carb/fibre totals, and every food row's per-100g macro line. Icons are decorative (aria-hidden); a visually-hidden .sr-only label sits next to each so screen readers still get "Protein 11 g," not just an icon.

Not applicable / already compliant, left untouched: no radial glow, vignette or luminous halo was present in the previous draft to begin with (the only gradient in the file is a top-fade behind the sticky calculator total, which exists purely so scrolled rows don't collide with the readout — not a decorative glow, so it stayed). No circular gauge or ring existed either — the kcal-remaining and volume-load indicators were always linear bars, so §3's "ring trap" warning didn't require a change here, only a radius fix (see below). The Training sparkline already sat directly on the background with zero gridlines and no legend (single series), so §2.4/§2.5 needed no edit; it has no daily cadence (it's an 8-week trend), so "axis reduced to day initials" doesn't apply to this particular chart.

Judgment call worth flagging: the binary radius rule was read strictly — "everything else flat" — so the linear progress bars (hero kcal bar, training volume bars) and the toast snackbar lost their pill ends and are now plain flat rectangles. This is a more literal reading than most mobile UI conventions (snackbars/progress tracks are almost always rounded); if that reads as too severe in practice, reintroducing a small fixed radius on non-actionable indicator-only elements (bars, toast) — while keeping it off every row/panel/card — would be a one-line, low-risk exception to revisit.

Still left out deliberately, unchanged from before: no manifest/service-worker wiring (this is a visual draft, not a build target), no real Hevy data for the Training screen (flagged in-page as illustrative), no attempt to reconcile this screen set with the live app's actual routing (ToC's non-mapped rows just toast "preview only"), and generic reference food values not yet sourced from Toni's actual labels.

Next step if this direction is picked: wire the citation stack up to the real back-navigation model already built in index.html (exact scroll-offset restore, not anchor-based), and pull real per-100g values for Toni's actual products before this touches the food calculator for real.
