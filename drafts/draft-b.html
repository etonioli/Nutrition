Energy balance

How the body gains and loses fat, what a calorie deficit actually does, and why weight loss slows down over time.

Evidence tier
2predominant · 6 sources
Certain–Likely
Not medical advice This section is general education, not personal medical advice. It describes what guideline bodies and published research say for general populations. It does not account for your individual medical history, and nothing here should be used to make a medical decision. Those belong with a physician.

Body weight change is governed by the balance between energy intake and energy expenditure . When expenditure exceeds intake, the body increases its oxidation of stored fat for fuel . When body weight changes because of an energy imbalance, roughly 60 to 80% of the resulting change in body mass is typically body fat — the remainder is other tissue and body water.

Where does the fat actually go?

When fat is metabolised for energy, most of its mass leaves the body as exhaled carbon dioxide, with the remainder excreted as water . Fat is not "burned off" as heat and it is not excreted — it is exhaled.

Why loss slows down

Weight loss produces changes in resting and non-resting energy expenditure that are independent of changes in fat-free mass — this is called adaptive thermogenesis . It is a real effect that can slow further loss and favour regain, but it is a temporary metabolic adaptation, not permanent or irreversible metabolic damage .

Not all calories cost the same to process

The thermic effect of food — the energy spent digesting and processing what you eat — is highest for protein at about 20 to 30% of ingested energy, moderate for carbohydrate at about 5 to 10%, and lowest for fat at about 0 to 3% .

Design notes

Revised against DESIGN-ANALYSIS.md (2026-07-31), which independently found this draft's clinical direction matches the real apps studied (asklepios, turing) most closely. Everything sits on bare background — no card borders, no shadows, no nested panels — except the ToC drawer, the one place a boxed container is allowed. Structure comes from hairline rules, uppercase micro-labels and tabular-numeral alignment instead. The food table is the strongest test of the concept: state (raw/dry/ready-to-eat) and source tier are surfaced per row precisely because they change what a number means.

Clinical metric-row pattern (§2.1+§2.3) now applied verbatim: label on its own line, a large value with a small muted unit beneath, and a dot + status word to the right — used for Home's Weight/Waist/Next-scan figures and the Reading page's evidence-tier callout. One radius token (a full pill) is now reserved strictly for actions and the search field — cite buttons, the add button, back-to-reading and the ToC search all take it; everything else (grams input, ToC box, muscle-group bars, toast) is flat, 0 radius. The lingering box-shadow on back-to-reading is gone — no shadow appears anywhere outside the ToC drawer. Chart legends use a dot + word (Training's weekly-volume chart), never a filled swatch; both Training charts sit on bare background with thin lines only, faint dotted gridlines at round values (1RM sparkline) and axis reduced to short date labels (volume bars). No circular gauges exist or were added — periodic scans and static targets don't earn a ring. Home and the calculator each carry exactly one oversized dominant figure (Weight; running-total kcal), with everything else on that screen deliberately quieter.

Left out deliberately: filled pill badges (replaced everywhere with a dot + text tag), any drop shadow outside the ToC drawer, and a manual light/dark toggle — this draft leans on prefers-color-scheme only. Food macro values and all Training-screen numbers except the two explicitly footnoted anchors (2026-07-27: 30 kg lat pulldown; 3,988 kg / 21 sets) are illustrative placeholders for layout purposes, not sourced data.

Next step if this direction is picked: replace the placeholder food macros with real BLS 4.0 / label figures per the project's sourcing gate, and wire the citation stack to real scroll-position persistence instead of this simplified single-level back button.
