Nutrition info

Protein

How much protein the evidence supports, why the number goes up in a deficit, and how to spread it across the day.

Baseline requirements

EFSA has set the Population Reference Intake for protein at 0.83 g per kg of body weight per day for adults. The US Recommended Dietary Allowance is 0.8 g per kg of body weight per day. Both figures describe the amount needed to avoid deficiency in a general adult population — not the amount that optimises training adaptations.

If you train

For most exercising individuals, a daily protein intake of 1.4 to 2.0 g per kg of body weight is sufficient to build and maintain muscle mass.

If you train while in a calorie deficit

During a calorie deficit, resistance-trained individuals may need higher protein intakes of about 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg per day to maximise retention of lean body mass. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that protein intakes above the RDA are associated with greater retention of lean body mass than consuming the RDA, with larger effects under energy restriction combined with resistance training.

Distribution across the day

For maximising muscle protein synthesis, the ISSN position stand recommends ingesting roughly 20 to 40 g of high-quality protein — about 0.25 to 0.40 g per kg of body mass — every three to four hours.

Design notes

This draft treats the page background itself as the canvas: no card, no shadow, no nested panel carries body text — hierarchy comes entirely from a serif/sans pairing, a single consistent measure, hairline rules, and generous vertical rhythm. The one container in the whole draft is the table-of-contents drawer, which earns its box because it's a transient overlay, not permanent chrome, and it's built with real indentation and hairline separators rather than a stacked list of boxes. Numbers (macro totals, 1RM, volume) are set as typographic statistics — large serif figure, small light-weight unit — so the "data" feels continuous with the reading experience rather than bolted on as a dashboard widget. Next, I'd push the citation system further: a slide-up reference drawer instead of a plain back-button, and pull the accent colour into the sparkline's axis labels so numbers and prose read as one voice. I'd also want real Skyr/ Vollkornbrot per-100g figures sourced from labels before this leaves draft status — the calculator's macro values below are illustrative placeholders, not yet BLS/USDA-sourced.
