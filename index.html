Timing rules

externally cited
  • The 30-minute post-workout anabolic window is not supported when a pre-training meal was eaten.
  • Protein and carbohydrate within ~2 hours after training is sufficient.
  • Within 60 minutes if training was fasted, or 5+ hours after the last meal.

Fat floor of 50 g ≈ 20% of calories. Below this, testosterone declines measurably and fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K are poorly absorbed. Treat 50 g as a floor, not a target to undercut.

Reading the numbers

required caveats
  • BIA infers muscle partly from body water. Muscle glycogen binds roughly 3 g water per gram of glycogen. Moving onto a low-carb protocol depletes glycogen, so a meaningful share of the −1.83 kg SMM change is glycogen and water, not contractile protein. Plausible true lean-tissue loss: 0.8-1.3 kg. Do not report the raw figure as if it were all muscle.
  • Torso is the least reliable BIA segment. It carried −1.22 kg of the −1.83 kg total, which is consistent with glycogen depletion rather than localised atrophy.
  • Creatine raises intracellular water and therefore inflates apparent SMM. If creatine was started between two measurements, real loss is worse than measured. Toni's creatine start date is not established — open backlog item.
  • VAT thresholds on this device: "Hoch" begins at 3.5 L. He is at 3.6 L — just above the boundary, down from ~4.1 L.

Phase 2 correction

highest-confidence finding

Toni was running Phase 1 rules while in Phase 2, on both axes:

CarbsPhase 2 explicitly prescribes carbohydrate with the evening meal, scaled to activity level. He was treating carbs as an occasional exception (3-4 days/week, unstructured).
FatPhase 1 caps oil at 1 tsp. Phase 2 specifies mediterranean meals, where olive oil and oily fish are foundational. He was still restricting to ~18 g fat/day.

Any future recommendation must be checked against the phase he is actually in, not the phase he started in. This is the highest-yield error pattern found so far.

Established findings

project brief

General physiology findings established so far, mostly presented as project-brief-level statements rather than pinned to a single external citation (the one exception is called out below).

  • Energy balance determines fat storage, not any individual macronutrient. In a deficit, no food is "stored as fat."
  • De novo lipogenesis is quantitatively minor in humans on normal intakes. Carbohydrate contributes to fat gain chiefly by sparing dietary fat from oxidation, not by converting to fat itself.
  • Dietary fat is stored most efficiently (~2-3% processing loss) because it needs no conversion. Protein is the least efficiently stored (20-30% thermic effect, plus expensive conversion).
  • Fructose and alcohol are the two dietary factors with the clearest link to visceral and hepatic fat, because both are metabolised primarily in the liver.
  • Visceral fat is the most responsive fat depot, with higher beta-adrenergic receptor density. It reduces proportionally faster than subcutaneous fat in early weight loss. Confirmed in his own data: −12% VAT in 18 days.
  • Fat leaves the body as CO2 and water, via lipolysis (ATGL and HSL) → beta-oxidation → Krebs cycle. Falling insulin is the permissive step.
  • The 30-minute post-workout anabolic window is not supported (Aragon & Schoenfeld 2013) when a pre-training meal was eaten — see Timing rules for the full statement.
  • Training intensity is the primary signal for muscle retention in a deficit. Carbohydrate exists in the plan to make that signal possible, which is why carbs track training volume.
  • Aerobic exercise has specific evidence for visceral fat reduction beyond its calorie cost. 2-3 walks of 20-30 min on rest days were suggested and remain unimplemented — a suggestion, not yet acted on.

Source: project brief / Toni's own record, except the post-workout timing point which is Aragon & Schoenfeld 2013 (see Timing rules).
